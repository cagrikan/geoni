ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity: Same Question, Different Answers

Last updated: 6 August 2026 · GEONI Team

The four AI engines do not answer the same question the same way. A brand named in a Perplexity answer and absent from a ChatGPT answer is an ordinary outcome. The reason is simple: each engine uses its own retrieval stack and its own logic for choosing sources. This is why AI visibility cannot be measured on one engine.

Three risks of looking at one engine

  1. False comfort. You appear in ChatGPT, so you assume you are fine — while being absent from Claude on the very same question.
  2. False alarm. Missing from one engine does not mean “AI doesn't know me”; that engine may simply choose sources differently.
  3. Wasted effort. Producing content without knowing which engine you are missing from means spending on ground you already hold.

Answers move between runs

Generative models can retrieve different pages on each run and compose the answer again. That is why a single screenshot is not evidence. What matters is a repeated question set and the trend in its results. A brand appearing one week and not the next is not a measurement error — it is the nature of this surface, and the direction of the trend is the signal.

Google's AI Overview is its own surface

The AI Overview box above Google's results can draw on a different set of sources than the classic ranking. Being first in classic search does not mean you appear in that box. The two are checked separately, and in a GEONI report they appear as separate lines.

A practical measurement routine

  1. Build a question set. 10–20 questions a real customer would ask: “best X”, “who does X”, “how to choose X”.
  2. Ask all four separately. Same question, same day, on each engine.
  3. Record two things: whether your brand appeared, and which sources the answer cited.
  4. Repeat. Weekly or monthly — one check is noise, the trend is signal.
  5. Confirm access. Allow GPTBot, ClaudeBot and PerplexityBot in robots.txt; if your page is drawn only with JavaScript, the model cannot read it.

Frequently asked questions

Which engine matters most?

It depends on your sector; there is no single answer. The right approach is not picking one but knowing where you stand on all four.

How many questions should I start with?

10–20 is enough. A small set of questions in your customers' actual words beats a large set of invented ones.

Can I do this by hand?

Yes — ask each engine and take notes. The difficulty is repetition: sustaining a weekly measurement and comparing source lists by hand takes time.
